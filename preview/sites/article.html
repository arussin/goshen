Notes on everyday design

The quiet work
of making room.

Sometimes a useful new idea begins with one less thing on the table.

Words by Alex Morgan · September 8 · 4 minute read

The first thing we moved was a shelf. It had been there so long that nobody remembered choosing it. Underneath were five notebooks, two unmatched cups, and a small rectangle of wall that had never seen the sun.

With the shelf gone, the room was not transformed. It was simply easier to notice. Morning light reached the desk. A chair could turn without bumping anything. There was somewhere to put a drawing down and leave it there.

Space is a useful material.

We tend to talk about making as a process of adding: one more feature, another color, an extra shelf. But space can do some of the work. A margin helps a sentence breathe. A quiet corner gives a bright object its shape.

Leave a little room for the thing you haven’t thought of yet.

This is not a rule about minimalism. Some rooms are better with more books, more plants, and more company. It is a question of attention. What do we want to see first? What is worth keeping close?

A small practice for Monday.

Pick one surface. Clear it, then bring back the things that earn their place. Let the experiment last a week. Notice what you reach for and what you forget. You can always put the shelf back.

In the studio, we wrote the idea as a tiny note to ourselves:

start with what matters
leave enough space to move
make one useful thing
repeat when ready

There is more from the workbench in our collection of small experiments. None of it is finished forever. That is part of the appeal.

A thought to keep.
